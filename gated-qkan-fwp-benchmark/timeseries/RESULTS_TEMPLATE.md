* Margins (mean test MSE, paper best / v2): 3e8x-2e11x on the four smooth datasets, 7e2x-6e5x on NARMA-5,
  22x-1.8e3x on NARMA-10. The two tightest cells are NARMA-10 N=16 (9.0e-8 vs 2.0e-6 / 2.55e-6) and NARMA-10 N=8
  (6.9e-7 vs 4.3e-5 / 2.25e-5).
* Parameters: 153 / 225 / 241 / 273 for N = 8 / 16 / 32 / 64 (v1: 159; follow-up: 209-306). A smaller variant with
  92-156 parameters (`v2_fwp159`) also wins all 24 cells.
* QKAN is load-bearing where the task is nonlinear: removing the QKAN layer (same pipeline, `v2_no_qkan`) raises the
  error 600x-3e5x on NARMA-5, 7x-400x on NARMA-10, 350x-2e4x on the damped pendulum and 9x-16x on Bessel; it makes
  no difference on delayed quantum control and Jaynes-Cummings, which the linear path alone already predicts to
  1e-14 / 1e-13. Without QKAN the pipeline loses one cell to the paper (NARMA-10 N=16: 4.0e-6 vs 2.0e-6).
* What v2 does NOT do: it does not beat the classical closed-form baselines (polynomial-lag ridge / kernel ridge,
  no QKAN) in 19 of the 24 cells, and replacing the DARUAN circuits by a classical tanh gives about the same
  accuracy (Section 3). The gain over v1 comes from giving the model the window, a closed-form linear path and
  read-out, and a well-conditioned optimisation; QKAN supplies the nonlinear part but is not uniquely required.

* **Objective.** Training on the training loss gives the same geometric mean (5.7e-13); it is 3x-10x better on

  chosen on the training hold-out for exactly that robustness (Section 5) and was not revisited after the test run.
* **Observable.** <Z> is 2.3x better than the package's fast measure in the geometric mean (mostly the pendulum).
* **Capacity.** Doubling h (297-481 parameters) improves the geometric mean 1.8x and comes within 1.1x-4.4x of

## 4. Method (`v2.py`, preset `v2_main`)

For a window x of N values (the protocol's float32 arrays, computed in float64):

    xs    = (x - mu) / sigma                       mu, sigma: scalar mean / std of all training-window entries
    z_j   = sum_{k in last K lags} P_jk g^(age_k) xs_k      j = 1..h;  K = min(N, 16), g = 0.8, age 0 = newest
    e_oj  = <Z> after  H, RZ(a0_oj), RY(b0_oj), RZ(z_j), RZ(a1_oj), RY(b1_oj)  on |0>     o = 1..m
    y_hat = b + sum_k l_k xs_k + sum_oj c_oj e_oj

* **QKAN part.** `e` is one QKAN layer `[h -> m]` = `[8 -> 2]`: 16 independent single-qubit data re-uploading
  circuits (DARUAN, qkan package, ansatz `pz_encoding`, reps = 1, no base activation), evaluated in closed form by
  rotating the Bloch vector. `test_v2.py` copies the parameters into `qkan.QKANLayer(solver="exact")` and proves the
  outputs identical (max abs difference 4.3e-6 in float32 over 8 layer configurations; whole model 2.9e-7).
  Observable: the physical <Z> (not the package's default fast measure). The coefficients c are the layer's
  post-activation weights. For reps = 1 and <Z> an edge is C + R cos(z + psi).
* **Encoded angles.** z are h learned linear combinations of the last K lags, parametrised on the lag-weighted input
  (g^age is a fading-memory prior: v1's gated memory kernel with a constant gate). Because RZ(a) RZ(z) = RZ(a + z),
  z can be read as an input-programmed shift of the circuit's RZ angle, but unlike v1 there is no input-dependent
  gate and the write is a position-dependent linear map; `v2_main` is a QKAN regressor, not a fast-weight
  programmer. (`v2_fwp159` adds a genuine gated programmer, see below.)
* **Read-out in closed form (variable projection).** (b, l, c) minimise
  `||y - b - F c||^2 + lambda n sum_j (c_j / q_j)^2` over the training windows, F = [edges, xs]; prior scales
  q = 1 / std(edge) for the QKAN edges and g^age for the lags; solved by SVD. Never trained by gradient.
* **Inner parameters (P and the circuit angles) by gradient.** Objective = forward-chaining validation loss on the
  training windows: with block size 0.15 n, for each of 3 folds the read-out is solved on windows [0, a) and scored
  on the next block [a, a + 0.15 n), the last block ending at n; the loss is the mean of the three block MSEs,
  differentiated through the ridge solution (QR of the augmented system). Optimiser: full-batch L-BFGS
  (`scipy` L-BFGS-B, memory 20) hard-capped at **50 function/gradient evaluations**; one evaluation computes the
  features of all training windows once (one epoch). The evaluated point with the lowest objective is kept, then the
  read-out is solved on all training windows.
* **Initialisation (the only use of the seed).** P Gaussian, each row rescaled so that z_j has std 0.1 over the
  training windows; RZ angles uniform in (-pi, pi), RY angles (0, pi/2), i.e. every edge starts as -cos(z + phase).
* **Parameter arithmetic.** P: h K = 8 min(N, 16); angles: 4 m h = 64; read-out: m h + N + 1 = 16 + N + 1.
  Total 153 / 225 / 241 / 273 for N = 8 / 16 / 32 / 64 (128 / 192 / 192 / 192 trained by gradient, the rest solved).
* **In-family variant `v2_fwp159`.** h = 4, K = 10, m = 2 and a gated fast-weight programmer added to the encoded
  angles: at every step t of the window a slow net reads c_t = (xs_t, xs_{t-1}), gate g_t = sigmoid(beta + w.c_t)
  (beta initialised at v1's +2), and `z += sum_t (1 - g_t) prod_{s>t} g_s W c_t` with W zero-initialised (v1's gated
  fold; `test_v2.py` checks it against the step-by-step recurrence). Parameters 92 / 108 / 124 / 156.

## 5. How every hyperparameter was selected

No test data was used for any choice. All design decisions were made with `v2_lab.py`, which fits on the first 80%
of the TRAINING windows and reports the MSE on the last 20% of the TRAINING windows only (it never passes test
arrays to a model or a metric). The recipe was frozen (`runs/timeseries/PROGRESS_v2.md`, milestone M2) before the
first test evaluation; `v2_main` was then evaluated on the test split once, and nothing was changed afterwards.

| quantity | value | how it was chosen |
|---|---|---|
| ridge lambda | per cell and seed, from 1e-14..1e-2 (13 values) | automatically inside the procedure: lowest MSE on the last 20% of the training windows with the read-out solved on the first 80%, at the initial QKAN parameters (no gradient step) |
| fading-memory decay g | 0.8 | hold-out probes p04 (N=64 fails without it), p14 (0.7 ~ 0.8, both better than 0.9), p07 and p11 (v1's 0.881 slightly worse) |
| angle scale at initialisation | 0.1 rad | probes p02, p04, p14 (0.03-0.3 equivalent within noise) |
| h, m, K | 8, 2, min(N, 16) | probes p10, p11, p13: accuracy per parameter; K = 16 ~ K = N |
| reps, observable | 1, <Z> | probes p06, p10, p11 (reps 2 no better), p13 (<Z> better than fast measure) |
| objective | forward-chaining, 3 folds x 15% | probes p05, p06, p08-p10: training-loss objective overfits the information-starved cells |
| evaluations | 50 | the benchmark's epoch budget (not tuned) |

Selected ridges in the final `v2_main` runs: 1e-14 (the edge of the grid) in 79 of the 80 smooth cell-seeds (1e-13
in the other); 1e-13..1e-3 on NARMA.
`v2_fwp159` was specified after the `v2_main` test run (sizes fixed by the 159-parameter budget, context length
and K compared on the training hold-out only, probe p16) and evaluated once. The ablation presets are single
switches of `v2_main` and were evaluated once each. Probes p01-p08 predate the lag-weighted parametrisation of P and
are kept as records (`runs/timeseries/v2_lab/*.json`); they cannot be regenerated exactly with the final code.

## 6. Protocol compliance

* Data, windows, chronological 80/20 split, scaling, metric (`protocol.test_mse`, final model), seeds 0-4, mean and
  population std: `protocol.py`, unchanged from v1 (`test_protocol.py`).
* Each seed is an independent repetition of the whole procedure (hold-out ridge choice, training, read-out).
* Gradient training: at most 50 full-batch function/gradient evaluations (119 of the 120 `v2_main` runs used all
  50, one stopped after 21).
  The ridge choice uses closed-form solves only. Each evaluation of the forward-chaining loss contains three
  closed-form ridge solves on prefixes of the training windows.
* Computation in float64 on the float32 protocol arrays. The targets are float32 numbers, so their rounding alone
  leaves an error of about (6e-8)^2 / 12 = 3e-16 for values near 1 (an estimate, not a measurement); the lowest
  MSEs in the tables (2e-16) are at that level.
* Runtime: 52 s for all 120 `v2_main` cell-seeds on one core (median 0.26 s, max 1.8 s).

## 8. Caveats

* The follow-up paper's numbers are a transcription by our literature scout and were not re-run.
* The benchmark is small and noise-free: 140-232 training windows on Bessel, pendulum and NARMA, 35-59 test windows;
  the NARMA input is a smooth deterministic signal, so the test split measures extrapolation in time along one
  trajectory. Differences of a factor 2-3 between presets are within seed-to-seed spread (see the stds).
* On delayed quantum control and Jaynes-Cummings the result is set by the linear path; QKAN adds nothing there.
  Why Jaynes-Cummings stops at 1.1e-13 for every model, including the classical ones, was not investigated.
* `v2_main` has more parameters than v1 for N >= 16 (225-273 vs 159).
* The NARMA-5 N=16 mean (6.0e-11) is dominated by one seed (2.3e-10; the other four are 5e-12..3e-11).
